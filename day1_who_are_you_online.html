📋 DLCS 6-8.CAS.b.1 — Digital Citizenship & Online Identity
🪞
What Is Online Identity?
The Big Idea
Your online identity is everything that represents you on the internet — your posts, comments, profile pictures, usernames, videos, likes, and even what others say about you online. It's the impression people form of you when they look you up.
Online Identity
The version of yourself you present on the internet — it can be curated, partial, or very different from who you are in real life.
Digital Persona
A character or image you intentionally create online — like a stage name, avatar, or consistent "brand" across platforms.
Audience
Everyone who can see your content — followers, strangers, future employers, even your grandma. You can't always control who's watching.
Personal Brand
The consistent message and image you build over time. Influencers do this on purpose — but everyone has one, even if they didn't choose it.
First Impression
Online, your first impression is what someone sees when they Google your name or look at your profile before they ever meet you in person.
Permanence
Once something is posted online, it's nearly impossible to fully delete. Screenshots, caches, and reposts can keep it alive forever.
Real vs. Online Self: Most influencers show a curated version of themselves — the highlights, the best lighting, the funniest moments. That gap between the real person and their online persona is something to think about critically.
Real-World Examples
🌟MrBeast built a persona around big stunts and charity. His online identity = generous, high-energy, extreme. But he's also a calculated businessman running a media company.
🔬Mark Rober built his identity around being "the science guy who makes it fun." Former NASA engineer — his credibility is a huge part of his brand.
☕Emma Chamberlain built a persona around being unfiltered and real. That authenticity IS her brand — but it's still a carefully built identity.

📋 DLCS 6-8.CAS.b.2 — Understanding Digital Footprints
🦶
What Is a Digital Footprint?
The Big Idea
A digital footprint is the trail of data you leave behind every time you use the internet. Some of it you leave on purpose. Some of it is collected without you even knowing.
🟢 Active Footprint
Data you intentionally share — you made a choice to put it out there.
• Posting a photo or video
• Writing a comment or caption
• Filling in your profile info
• Tagging someone in a post
• Signing up with your real name
• Sending a direct message
🔵 Passive Footprint
Data collected about you — you didn't actively choose to share it.
• Your location tracked by an app
• Search history saved by Google
• Watch history on YouTube
• Website cookies stored in your browser
• Ads targeted based on what you click
• Which posts you scroll past and stop on
⚠️ The sneaky part: You might think you're being careful, but passive data collection happens in the background 24/7 — on every app, every website, and every device you use. Companies use this data to build a profile of you and sell it to advertisers.
Why It Matters — Real Consequences
💼Jobs: 70% of employers say they check social media before hiring. Things posted in middle school can show up in a search years later.
🎓College: Some colleges now look at applicants' social media. A single controversial post has gotten students rejected.
💸Money: Your data is worth money. Tech companies profit from selling your behavior profile to advertisers — you're the product.
